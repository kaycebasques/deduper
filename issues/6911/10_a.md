> What's the difference between the two?

At the high level, as described in the linked intersphinx docs and as Adam and I mentioned elsewhere, using `:external:` explicitly specifies using an external Intersphinx inventory, whereas for the normal roles it's a little more implicit, as it rather falls back to an intersphinx lookup after the target is not found internally. However, with an explicitly specified inventory for both, in practice they produce the same results in the rendered output outside of pathological cases.

Examining [the code](https://github.com/sphinx-doc/sphinx/blob/master/sphinx/ext/intersphinx.py#L496), you can see the two take different codepaths, at least until they get to the lower-level lookup—the `:external:` role is handled by the [`IntersphinxRole` class](https://github.com/sphinx-doc/sphinx/blob/master/sphinx/ext/intersphinx.py#L485), while the normal xref role lookups are handled by the [`resolve_reference_detect_inventory()` function](https://github.com/sphinx-doc/sphinx/blob/master/sphinx/ext/intersphinx.py#L435), which gets called when local lookup does not succeed.

Specifically, using the normal roles first tries a local lookup, then an all-inventory intersphinx lookup of the fully qualified name, and finally (if an inventory name is prefixed) falling back to a lookup of the prefix-stripped target in the specified Intersphinx inventory. However, for explicit inventory names, adding an Intersphinx target name prefix directly implies it will be an Intersphinx lookup and de facto ensures that (outside of pathological corner cases) the target will not be resolved locally and will fall back to Intersphinx.

So, there is a likely small amount of efficiency and robustness benefit with `external` from not doing the local lookup first and performing a couple extra checks (the possible performance win of which may or may not outweigh the extra logic, check and dispatch overhead, and is likely close to negligible in practice). However, IMO, that possible optimization isn't generally worth the effort or complexity for authors writing the source or others reading it, given the trouble that non-docs-gurus seem to have reading and writing normal reST syntax as it is.
