> Sphinx LaTeX is now quite sophisticated at various levels:
> 
> * Python: `writers/latex.py` has `2491` lines and `writers/builders/latex/*` cumulate `1759` lines, the templates cumulate `311` lines
> 
> * LaTeX: the `.sty` and `.cls` files accumulate about `6900` lines of LaTeX code.
> 
> There are also `1181` lines of lisp code (very repetitive) for `xindy`, and the provided support for `latexmk` (also for Japanese context) and `make`.

That is far more complex than the Docutils LaTeX writer with 3389 lines supported by
4 templates with ~20 lines each and `docutils.sty` with 247 lines. 
The Docutils LaTeX writer deliberately tries a "keep it simple" approach to be usable with a wide range of LaTeX documentclasses and packages without requiring more that the basic ones.

Unfortunately, the Sphinx and Docutils LaTeX writers diverged a long time ago and developed in different directions. 
This means that my solutions for Docutils can't work 1:1 in the Sphinx writer:
I will scale down my suggestions, ignore any part that does not fit. 
 
> I am throwing all these figures only to stress that it may be difficult to follow suit if the layer below were to experience drastic evolutions.

Generally, Docutils tries to keep the core API as stable as possible 
and announce incompatible changes in the [RELEASE-NOTES](https://docutils.sourceforge.io/RELEASE-NOTES.html). 
Problems arise with creative use of implementation details and  urgent bugfixes.
Early feedback helps to find workable solutions.
