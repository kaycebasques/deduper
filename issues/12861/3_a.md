> I'm wondering what could have been the motivation for case-insensitivity in intersphinx? Maybe because intersphinx artifacts could become [part of the URL](https://stackoverflow.com/questions/24699643/are-query-string-keys-case-sensitive)? But in practice they're always part of the query string?!

I always seem to forget that it's only _some_ Intersphinx entries that are resolved case-insensitively (`std:label` and `std:term` specifically).

The code [contains some relevant comments](https://github.com/sphinx-doc/sphinx/blob/04381789db7466d56d9eb29d23d979fc16604acc/sphinx/ext/intersphinx/_resolve.py#L78-L80) -- re-reading those I infer:

  * Intersphinx was, I think (but I'm not 100% sure) originally designed with entirely case-sensitive matching.  However: Sphinx would emit warnings when projects defined duplicative terms (and labels?).
  * However, in contrast, `term` definitions in Sphinx were case-insensitive -- my [best-guess](https://github.com/sphinx-doc/sphinx/issues/12613#issuecomment-2241160387) is that that was to make term cross-referencing slightly more convenient.
  * Bugreport #7418 relates to a project that has a genuine requirement for two case-insensitively-equal strings -- `MySQL` and `mysql` -- to be [defined as separate terms](https://github.com/phpmyadmin/phpmyadmin/blob/b8689553c85f92819b9c97ce558277d19af04e5e/docs/glossary.rst?plain=1#L239-L253).  As a result, `term` began to support -- but does not require -- case-sensitive matching.
  * In #9291, Intersphinx's handling of resolution of `term` references was reported as inconsistent with Sphinx's internal `term` resolution -- and so Intersphinx resolution was adjusted to allow case-insensitive resolution.
